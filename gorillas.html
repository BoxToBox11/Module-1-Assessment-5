GORILLAS

Gorillas are large primates of the family Hominidae and the order Primates. Two species are traditionally recognised, the western gorilla (Gorilla gorilla) and the eastern gorilla (Gorilla beringei), with genetic evidence dividing them into distinct subspecies like the western lowland, Cross River, mountain, and Grauer's gorillas. Gorillas are scattered throughout the tropical forests of equatorial Africa. Hominidae is the family of great apes, which includes gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, orangutans, and humans. Adult male gorillas, known as silverbacks, are the largest extant primates and can reach a standing height of 1.75 m (5 ft 9 in) and weigh up to 200 kg (440 lb).

All gorillas have several distinctive features, the most notable of which is a powerfully built, broad chest and long, muscular arms used for knuckle-walking and climbing. Their opposable thumbs and big toes allow for a strong grip, serving as excellent tools for gathering food and stripping bark. Gorillas' large, prominent sagittal crests on their skulls support massive jaw muscles. Their thick fur coats help to control their body temperature in cold mountain climates. Eastern gorillas have longer, darker fur and broader faces, while western gorillas have shorter, lighter hair and narrower faces.

Gorillas are predominantly herbivorous and can be found in different habitats including rainforests, swamps, and montane forests. They prefer to stay near dense foliage rich in stems, bamboo shoots, and wild fruits.
