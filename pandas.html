PANDAS

Pandas are large mammals of the family Ursidae and the order Carnivora. Two subspecies are traditionally recognised, the Sichuan giant panda (Ailuropoda melanoleuca melanoleuca) and the Qinling giant panda (Ailuropoda melanoleuca qinlingensis), with genetic evidence showing distinct differences in their skull shape and coat colour. Pandas are scattered throughout a few mountain ranges in central China, mainly in Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu provinces. Ursidae is the bear family; other, still surviving, members of the family include grizzly bears, polar bears, and black bears. Adult male giant pandas are typically larger than females and can reach a shoulder height of 0.9 m (3 ft) and weigh up to 160 kg (350 lb).

All pandas have several distinctive features, the most notable of which is a unique "pseudo-thumb," an extension of the wrist bone used as a tool for grasping and stripping bamboo stems. Their massive jaws and strong molar teeth serve as powerful tools for crushing tough plant stalks. Pandas' thick, woolly coats help to control their body temperature in cold, damp mountain climates. Their heavy, stocky bodies carry their weight through dense undergrowth. Sichuan pandas have the classic stark black-and-white markings, while Qinling pandas have smaller skulls and a unique dark brown-and-light brown coat pattern.

Pandas are herbivorous carnivorans that feed almost exclusively on bamboo, and can be found in different habitats including high-altitude montane forests and bamboo groves. They prefer to stay near misty, high-elevation areas with plenty of fresh water and dense canopy cover.
