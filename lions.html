LIONS

Lions are large felines of the family Felidae and the order Carnivora. Two subspecies are traditionally recognised, the African lion (Panthera leo leo) and the Asiatic lion (Panthera leo persica), though modern taxonomy groups them into northern and southern clades. Lions are scattered throughout parts of sub-Saharan Africa and a single remnant population in western India. Felidae is the family of cats; other, now extinct, members of the Panthera genus include the American lion and the cave lion. Adult male lions are the second-largest extant felids and can reach a shoulder height of 1.2 m (4 ft) and weigh up to 250 kg (550 lb).

All lions have several distinctive features, the most notable of which is a muscular, deep-chested body and a tufted tail, used for balance and communication. Male lions grow a prominent mane, which serves as protection during fights and as a visual display of health and maturity. Their retractable claws and powerful forelimbs serve as weapons for bringing down large prey. Lions' short, tawny coats help them camouflage in dry grasses. African lions typically have larger, fuller manes while Asiatic lions have smaller manes and a distinctive, longitudinal fold of skin running along their bellies.

Lions are apex carnivores and can be found in different habitats including savannahs, grasslands, dense brush, and semi-arid deserts. They prefer to stay near waterholes where prey animals gather.
